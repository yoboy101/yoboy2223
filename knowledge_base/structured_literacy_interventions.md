# Structured literacy and multisensory intervention

Tags: intervention, multisensory, structured literacy, Orton-Gillingham, fluency, comprehension, progress monitoring

## Principles
- Structured literacy teaches phonology, sound-symbol links, syllables, morphology, syntax and meaning explicitly and in a planned order.
- Multisensory practice links seeing, saying, hearing and writing the same item (see-say-trace-write).
- Lessons are short, frequent and cumulative, with immediate corrective feedback.

## Fluency
- Repeated reading: the learner reads the same short passage three or four times; record words correct per minute each time.
- Paired reading: adult and learner read together; the learner signals when ready to read alone.

## Comprehension
- Ask who, what, where, when and why questions after each short passage.
- Teach retelling with a simple story map (beginning, middle, end).

## Spelling
- Spell by sounds first (segment, then write one grapheme per sound).
- Practise high-frequency irregular words with the heart-word method: mark the irregular part with a heart and learn it by heart.

## Progress monitoring
- Reassess every 4 to 6 weeks with the same type of task (letter sounds, word reading, short passage).
- If progress is flat after two cycles, change the intervention and refer for specialist assessment.
