# South African classroom context

Tags: South Africa, CAPS, Foundation Phase, home language, multilingual, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi, Afrikaans, referral

## Curriculum
- In CAPS Foundation Phase (Grades R to 3) learners are taught to read in their home language, with First Additional Language (often English) introduced alongside it.
- Home-language reading lessons include phonics, shared reading, group guided reading and independent reading.

## Language considerations
- Many African languages (for example isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana) have transparent, consistent spelling and are agglutinating: long words built from many syllables and morphemes. Syllable-based decoding is therefore very important.
- Reading difficulty in English as an additional language is not the same as dyslexia. Check reading in the home language before concluding there is a specific learning difficulty.
- Feedback to learners and parents should be given in the home language wherever possible; a teacher or speaker of the language should check machine-generated text.

## Referral pathway
- A screening tool flags risk only. Next steps are the school-based support team (SBST), then the district-based support team, and assessment by an educational psychologist or speech-language therapist.
- Hearing and vision should be checked before a reading difficulty is attributed to dyslexia.
