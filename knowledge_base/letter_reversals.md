# Letter reversals (b/d, p/q, s, z, numbers)

Tags: reversal, b/d confusion, p/q, mirror writing, orientation, handwriting

Reversing letters is common in children up to about age 7 or 8. Persistent reversals after Grade 2, together with slow decoding and poor spelling, are a reason to look more closely, but reversals on their own do not prove dyslexia.

## Strategies
- Teach one letter of a confusable pair first (for example b) until it is secure, then introduce d. Do not teach b and d in the same lesson.
- Use a consistent verbal path for letter formation: "b: bat first, then the ball" (line down, then the bump); "d: start with the c (the door handle), then the line up".
- Anchor words: keep a picture card for one anchor word per letter (bed: the word looks like a bed when b and d sit at each end).
- Multisensory tracing: trace the letter in sand, on a textured card or in the air while saying the sound.
- Sort activities: learners sort word cards or picture cards by starting sound /b/ or /d/.
- Self-check: after writing, the learner checks each b or d against the anchor card.
- Keep practice short (5 to 10 minutes) and daily rather than long and occasional.

## Example Grade 1-2 activity
"Bed check": write five short words that start with b or d (bat, dog, bus, dig, bed). The learner reads each word, points to the first letter, says its sound, and checks it against the bed card.
