# Core phonics rules for early readers

Tags: phonics, short vowels, CVC words, digraphs, blending, segmenting, decoding

## Short vowels
- Closed syllables (a vowel followed by a consonant) usually have a short vowel: cat, pen, sit, hot, cup.
- Teach short vowels with a key picture and a mouth position: a (apple), e (egg), i (insect), o (octopus), u (umbrella).

## CVC blending and segmenting
- Blend: say each sound and slide them together (/c/ /a/ /t/ -> cat).
- Segment: tap one finger per sound while saying the word slowly.
- Use sound boxes (Elkonin boxes): one box per sound, push a counter into each box as the sound is said.

## Digraphs
- Two letters, one sound: sh, ch, th, wh, ck. Teach after single consonants and short vowels are secure.

## Silent e (magic e)
- A final e usually makes the vowel say its name: cap -> cape, kit -> kite, hop -> hope.

## Practice principles
- Explicit, systematic and cumulative: each new rule builds on rules already learned.
- Mix decoding (reading) and encoding (spelling) of the same pattern in each lesson.
- Use decodable text that only contains taught patterns.
